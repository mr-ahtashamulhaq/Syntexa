First let’s separate required questions
Q: Which method do you recommend and which do you reject?
I recommend Dynamic Tokens (OTP) and reject Retina/Iris Scanning as the primary
method. This choice is not based on which technology performs better, but on which
failure can be undone.
Q: Which "unrecoverable risk" is more acceptable?
The risk of a False Negative locking out a sysadmin is more acceptable. That situation has
workarounds, a second admin, an emergency override, a supervisor call. The harm ends by
morning.
Q: Why is biometric failure considered "unrecoverable"?
If the biometric database is hacked, the employee cannot change their iris, ever. That
stolen pattern follows them into banking, border control, and national ID systems for the
rest of their life. The University caused permanent harm to a person who had no choice but
to trust it.
Q: Why is the OTP failure considered "recoverable"?
If a token system is compromised, the University simply revokes the old token and issues a
new one. The employee's identity is untouched. The incident is contained, ﬁxed, and
closed.
Q: Why does the University's culture and context matter here?
The University is still upgrading its security, meaning it has not yet proven it can safely
protect something as sensitive and permanent as biological data. Until that maturity is
reached, it should not hold something it cannot give back if things go wrong.
